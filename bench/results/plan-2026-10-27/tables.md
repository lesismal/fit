# Load test plan, 2026-10-27: the tables

The report: [English](report.md) | [简体中文](report.zh-CN.md)

The tables of every workload of [the load test plan](../../README.md#the-load-test-plan), as benchreport
renders them from the JSON in this folder, one file for each workload and level: 232 runs on the
benchmark host, 2026-10-26 18:51 to 20:23 UTC, fit at c08eb0d (the harness's uncommitted changes
being what `+dirty` names) with fib at 33a039f1, every run 5s warm-up and 15s measured, none
failed, none hung, no errors in any. The plan's sizes were derived by `fitbench plan`
([plan.txt](plan.txt)): levels 100 to 10,000 requests in flight; the server under test on CPUs
`0-2,8-10` (6 workers), the backends on `3-4,11-12` (4 CPUs), the load generators on `5-7,13-15`
(6 threads); W6 calibrated to 30,000 and 200,000 a second. A cell is the successful responses a
second and their share of the best.

The backends never set the pace: the proxy backend used at most 3.07 of its 4 CPUs (W1 at 100,
nginx over HTTP/1.1), 2.99 in W5 and 2.94 in W4, and the cache origin at most 0.01. The servers
under test used 5.4-6.0 of their 6 CPUs in W0-W2, W4 and W5, and 4.0-5.9 in W3, where the TLS
handshake sets the pace.

Every table has the servers' memory: peak anonymous memory during the run, and MEM EER, successful
responses a second for each MiB of it; the scaling tables add peak memory at each level.
