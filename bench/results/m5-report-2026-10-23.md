# M5 report, 2026-10-23

Benchmarks at the end of M5 (caching and extensions, I67–I75), on the same Linux host and with the
same harness settings as the [M4 report](m4-report-2026-10-23.md), with fit at commit 9434ecb and
fib at `3998953` (A40, on fib's branch `a40-filter-abort`). M5's exit criteria hold:

- **The cache against nginx's `proxy_cache`** (I69): on the harness's cache scenarios, with caches
  that hold the working set, fit keeps what nginx keeps (99.99% of requests answered from the cache
  in the Zipf scenario, against nginx's 99.85%), at about twice nginx's rate over HTTP/1.1 and five
  times over h2c; a stampede of 100 clients on a cold key makes one fetch (`TestStampede`).
- **A WASM plugin that edits headers** (I71) adds 4.8 µs a request, bounded by its pool's
  instances and queue, its call's timeout and its instances' memory.
- **The nginx converter** (I74) converts its sample configs and reports every directive it skips,
  at its line.

This report is the full harness that rule 21 asks for at a milestone's end, now with the cache
scenarios; the modules' micro-benchmarks; and an A/B of M4 against M5.

- fit still meets every M1 target in the [roadmap](../../docs/roadmap.md#performance-targets), and
  measures as at M4 on fixed, static and proxy (A/B below).
- Idle connections cost fit what they did at M4, or a little less.
- **HTTP/3**, added to the harness afterwards ([below](#http3)): fit ties nginx on static and is
  12% and 17% behind it on fixed and proxy, and 1.8 to 5.3 times Caddy, Traefik and Envoy.

### Notes

In `cache-zipf` (100,000 paths by Zipf's law), 99.85% of nginx's requests and 99.99% of fit's were
answered from the cache: both hold every path, and what reaches the backend is the paths first asked
for during the run, fewer for fit, whose lock makes one fetch of a path asked for by several clients
at once. I69's report has the run with 64 MiB caches, which nginx could not be held to.

nginx's fixed p99 over HTTP/1.1 was 1.91 ms this time against 0.34 at M4, at the same throughput;
its mean was 0.22 ms.

In `cache-zipf` fit holds its 430 MB of responses in its own memory, and the Go heap's headroom on
top; nginx holds them on disk, in the page cache, which anonymous memory does not count.

## HTTP/3

The harness gained HTTP/3 after M5's runs: every server serves the same routes on UDP port 8444,
over TLS 1.3 with the harness's self-signed certificate, driven by h2load 1.68 built with HTTP/3
([`loadgen/Dockerfile.h3`](../loadgen/Dockerfile.h3)), with the same 64 connections of 32 streams as
h2c. Envoy 1.40.0 joined the comparison at the same time. These runs used fit at ed5ceb3 on fib
v1.6.0, later than M5's 9434ecb. Same host, CPUs, durations and server images as above. The
[v1.0.0 report](v1.0.0-report-2026-10-23.md) has the details.

Before the second run, the host's `net.core.rmem_max` and `wmem_max` went from 16384 to 7500000,
permanently (`/etc/sysctl.d/90-fitbench-udp-buffers.conf`). Under the old cap, quic-go (in Caddy
and Traefik), asking for 7 MB buffers, had latencies of up to 15 seconds. The tables are the
second run; their last column is the change from the first.

### fit's goroutines

Counted from its Admin API's `go_goroutines` every 100ms: 64 a second after start, at most
1,354 on fixed, 921 on static and 1,481 on proxy (2,694 on proxy in the first run). Over h2c the
most was 1,638, and 10,000 idle connections add none
([v1.0.0 report](v1.0.0-report-2026-10-23.md#fits-goroutines)).

HTTP/3 is where fit's lead is smallest. Its fixed rate over HTTP/3 is under half its h2c rate, and
its goroutines under HTTP/3 proxy vary from run to run. Both are worth a profile on the host.
