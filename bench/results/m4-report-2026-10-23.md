# M4 report, 2026-10-23

Benchmarks at the end of M4 (platform integration, I56–I66), on the same Linux host and with the
same harness settings as the [M3 report](m3-report-2026-10-22.md), with fit at commit 0dd5956 and
fib's `main` at 86071b8. M4's exit criteria are functional and hold: fit passes the Gateway API's
conformance suite for the core HTTP profile (I61), and the Docker Compose example routes by labels
and follows its containers as they start and stop (I66, run by CI). This report is the full harness
that rule 21 asks for at a milestone's end, the middleware's micro-benchmarks, and an A/B of M3
against M4.

- fit still meets every M1 target in the [roadmap](../../docs/roadmap.md#performance-targets).
- M4 is control plane work, and the data plane measures as at M3 but for h2c, where fit is 1.5% to
  1.8% slower (fixed and proxy, in an A/B of M3's build against M4's). No single change causes it:
  bisected, it builds up over I59–I64 in steps each within noise, while the code those iterations
  link in (providers, the Kubernetes, Consul and etcd clients, shared storage, the dashboard) grew
  fit's text by 4.5%. None of them changed what a request runs, as far as the bisection and the
  router's micro-benchmark show. It is recorded here (rule 22); I70's compile-time modules, which
  can leave the providers out of a build, are the place to measure it again.
- Idle connections cost fit a little more than at M3 at 10k (HTTP/1.1 1.4 KiB against 1.2, TLS
  9.3 against 9.1, WebSocket 6.0 against 5.5), still far below Caddy and Traefik, and below nginx
  but for HTTP/1.1.

## The h2c slowdown

Twenty commits lie between M3 and M4, and two of them change what a request runs: I65 (`c8ffc8f`)
checks for a tracer in the dispatcher, and `2839742` moves fib from `4f1c3e9` to `86071b8` (A38,
and fib's prefork and eBPF reuseport balancing, which nothing in fit enables). Neither is it:

| A | B | What B adds | fixed h2c | proxy h2c |
| --- | --- | --- | ---: | ---: |
| 740a4db | 2839742 | fib 86071b8 | +0.4% | +1.0% |
| e4579bb | c8ffc8f | I65 | +0.5% | −0.5% |

Bisected over fixed h2c, where it shows most:

| A | B | Iterations | fixed h2c |
| --- | --- | --- | ---: |
| 1486c28 (M3) | 15f6620 | I56–I58 | +0.1% |
| 15f6620 | 4f80a5a | I59–I61 | −0.8% |
| 4f80a5a | e4579bb | I62–I64 | −0.8% |

Each step is within noise, and together they are the 1.6% fit lost. In that range only I61 changed
the request path, `router.Match`, which looks for the Gateway API's `**.` wildcard hosts; its
micro-benchmark, I58 against I61, did not move (56.5 ns against 56.0, p=0.21, no allocations).
I63's dispatcher change is inside the ACME challenge branch, which no benchmark request reaches.
What did change is how much code fit links: its text grew from 5.83 MB at M3 to 6.14 MB at I58,
6.42 MB at I64 and 6.46 MB now, as M4 added the providers, their clients, shared storage and the
dashboard. The growth is consistent with code layout costing h2c's tightest loop a little, but does
not prove it: I56–I58 grew the text by 5% at no cost. The slowdown is recorded rather than fixed,
since nothing on the request path is there to fix; I70, `fit build` with compile-time modules, will
build fit without the providers, and is where to measure it again.

At 10k, fit's whole difference is 13 to 91 MiB, and what it gained since M3 is 1.4 MiB (HTTP/1.1),
2.2 (TLS) and 4.2 (WebSocket), amounts the Go heap's growth, which comes in steps, can account for;
at 100k, where a step weighs a tenth as much a connection, HTTP/1.1 is unchanged and TLS is 0.3 KiB
higher. Nothing M4 added runs per connection; the cause was not looked into further. At 100k the WebSocket run stopped at 64,501 connections, as at M3, when
fit's connections to the harness's one WebSocket backend address ran out of local ports. No server
closed a connection.
