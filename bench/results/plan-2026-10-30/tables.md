# Load test plan, 2026-10-30: the tables

The report: [English](report.md) | [简体中文](report.zh-CN.md)

The tables of every workload of [the load test plan](../../README.md#the-load-test-plan), as benchreport
renders them from the JSON in this folder, one file for each workload and level: 232 runs on the
benchmark host, 2026-10-30 11:25 to 12:57 UTC, fit at 24987e5 with fib at 7dcb41da, every run 5s
warm-up and 15s measured, none failed, none hung, no errors in any. The plan's sizes were derived by
`fitbench plan` ([plan.txt](plan.txt)): levels 100 to 10,000 requests in flight; the server under test
on CPUs `0-2,8-10` (6 workers), the backends on `3-4,11-12` (4 CPUs), the load generators on
`5-7,13-15` (6 threads); W6 calibrated to 30,000 and 200,000 a second. The host ran with its kernel
settings as they are, not the 2026-10-27 plan's: `somaxconn` 32,768 and so a listen backlog of 32,768
for every server, and larger default TCP buffers; [report.md](report.md#the-test-environment) lists
them, and [host-settings.sh](host-settings.sh) sets them. A cell is the successful responses a second
and their share of the best.

The backends never set the pace: the proxy backend used at most 2.99 of its 4 CPUs (W1 at 2,000,
nginx over HTTP/1.1). The servers under test used on average 5.7-6.0 of their 6 CPUs in W0-W2 and
W5, 5.4 in W4, where nginx's HTTP/1.1 download is limited by the load generators, and 5.1 in W3,
where the TLS handshake sets the pace.

Every table has the servers' memory: peak anonymous memory during the run, and MEM EER, successful
responses a second for each MiB of it; the scaling tables add peak memory at each level.
