# 025: Peer-to-peer connections with ICE, STUN and TURN, built in fit

[English](025-p2p.md) | [简体中文](025-p2p.zh-CN.md)

Status: accepted (2026-11-01).

## Context

fit is to connect two hosts that are both behind NAT: a service on one is reached from the other
directly, through a hole punched in both NATs, and through a relay where no hole can be punched (a
symmetric NAT on both sides, or UDP blocked). TCP and UDP services are both carried. The peers
interoperate with other agents: address discovery, connectivity checks and relaying follow ICE
(RFC 8445), STUN (RFC 8489) and TURN (RFC 8656), so a fit peer can use a public STUN server or a
coturn relay, and fit's own STUN and TURN servers serve other agents.

## Decision

- **fit implements the protocols itself, on fib, with no new dependency.** The Go implementations
  that exist (pion's `ice`, `stun` and `turn`) read their sockets on goroutines of their own,
  blocking in `ReadFrom`, and keep their timers on goroutines too; fit's rule 1 keeps every socket
  on fib's event loops, and two network stacks in one process would double the limits and metrics
  to keep. The protocols are small: STUN is a framing and a handful of attributes, TURN a table of
  allocations, permissions and channels, and ICE a checklist driven by timers.
- **Packages.** `p2p/stun` (messages and the STUN server and client), `p2p/turn` (the server and
  client), `p2p/ice` (the agent) and `p2p` (tunnels over a connected pair, signaling, and the
  config's apps). Each builds on the one before and can be used alone.
- **One socket per agent, demultiplexed by the first byte** as RFC 7983 and RFC 9443 do: STUN in
  0-3, TURN ChannelData in 64-79 and only from a TURN server's address, QUIC otherwise. The NAT's
  mapping belongs to the local port, so the checks, the relay's traffic and the payload must share
  it.
- **The payload is QUIC over the selected pair**, with fib's `quic` package, which already runs
  over any path a caller hands it (`quic.Dial(pc PacketConn, ...)`, `Conn.HandlePathDatagram`). TLS
  1.3 authenticates the peers by certificate fingerprints exchanged with the candidates, as SDP's
  `a=fingerprint` does for DTLS. A TCP connection is one bidirectional stream; a UDP flow is QUIC
  DATAGRAM frames (RFC 9221) with a varint flow ID in front, as RFC 9298 does with its context ID.
  QUIC gives congestion control, loss recovery and path migration, which a raw punched path lacks.
- **The relay is TURN.** The rendezvous fit runs a TURN server; ICE gathers relayed candidates and
  falls back to them when no direct pair succeeds, and keeps checking the direct ones. TURN's
  long-term credentials and the time-limited credentials of coturn's `use-auth-secret` (the TURN
  REST API draft) are both accepted.
- **Signaling is the application's in ICE; fit's is an endpoint on the rendezvous fit** that trades
  each side's ICE credentials, fingerprint and candidates (in RFC 8839's `candidate` syntax). Its
  shape is decided in I92.
- **Everything is bounded** (rule 11): STUN messages (to 1280 bytes over UDP, 4 KiB over TCP), the
  attributes of one message, TURN allocations in all and per user, permissions and channels per
  allocation, relayed bytes per second, ICE candidates and check pairs (RFC 8445's 100), and every
  transaction has a timeout (rule 10).
- **Passwords are taken as bytes.** RFC 8489 asks for the OpaqueString profile (RFC 8265) on
  passwords; fit does not apply it, so a password that is not ASCII must be given already in that
  form. Every implementation fit interoperates with treats ASCII passwords the same.

## What fib needs

- [A49](../fib-api-requests.md#a49-a-udp-peer-opened-toward-an-address-on-a-listeners-socket): a UDP
  peer opened toward an address on a listener's socket, before that address has sent anything.
  Hole punching sends first, from the port the NAT mapped, and a TURN allocation sends to its
  permitted peers from its relayed address.
- [A50](../fib-api-requests.md#a50-quic-datagram-frames): QUIC DATAGRAM frames, for UDP flows.
